This page exists so you can check our work before you pay for it. The universe is listed. The parameters are published. The results are stated as a range rather than a best case, and the one crash this model failed to protect against is described in as much detail as the ones it handled.

What you receive

A portfolio of four ETFs, reviewed once a month.

Four US-listed ETFs, equally weighted at 25% each, reviewed every 21 trading sessions. When the portfolio changes you receive the new holdings and the trades required to get there, through a private Telegram channel.

Between reviews there is nothing to do and nothing is sent. Long stretches of silence are the normal state of this model, not a gap in the service.

The universe

Thirty-two liquid ETFs. Fixed.

The model may only ever hold these, and the list does not change. Every one was already trading before the backtest begins — which is why survivorship bias, the flaw that quietly inflates most published backtests, cannot occur here.

The rules

Momentum decides what is eligible. Reversal decides what is bought.

At each review, the eight ETFs with the strongest twelve-month return are selected — excluding the most recent month. That exclusion is deliberate: the last month behaves differently from the eleven before it.

Among those eight, the four that have fallen most over the previous five sessions are held. The model buys strength that has just paused, not strength that has just run.

A regime filter sits above both. When the benchmark trades below its 200-day average at a review date, the portfolio moves entirely to cash and stays there until a later review says otherwise.

Equal weights. Long only. No leverage. No discretion at any point.

How the results are measured

The assumptions that work against the strategy, included.

Costs of 5 basis points per side on every trade.

Cash earns the real 3-month USD rate whenever the model is out of the market. Omitting this would understate a strategy that spends time in cash — and most published backtests omit it.

Signals are computed on closing prices and acted on the following session.

Period: 28 September 2007 to 18 September 2026 — the full history available for this universe, 2008 included.

These are backtested results. They are not an executed track record: no orders were placed and no fills occurred.

The exception

The 2020 crash is the one this model did not protect against, and the reason is structural rather than accidental. The portfolio is reviewed every 21 sessions, so a collapse that completes inside four weeks finds it fully invested with no opportunity to react.

It lost 27.9% against the index's 33.8% — protection, but not much of it. It finished that year up 23.4%.

Why we publish a range, not that number

A single parameter set can flatter any strategy. We tested 54 neighbouring sets — varying the lookbacks, the pool size and the number of positions — and the frozen set sits at the 91st percentile of that distribution.

Treat the median as the honest expectation. There is no reason the future should draw the same percentile as the past, and a service that quotes only its best configuration is telling you about its luck rather than its method.

What this is not

General signals. Not advice.

These are general signals, identical for every subscriber. They are not personalised investment advice, they are not tailored to your circumstances, risk tolerance or objectives, and we do not know what else you hold.

We make no forecasts and promise no returns. This strategy is not built to beat the index in a rising market — it did so in 11 of 20 years — but to lose substantially less when the market falls. Judge it over a full cycle, not a quarter.

Past performance and backtested results do not guarantee future results. You can lose money, including on positions shown in the channel.
